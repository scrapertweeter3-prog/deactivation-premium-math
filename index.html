Six weeks of pay belongs in the hourly rate

Gig listings quote a rate that assumes the account stays alive all year. The receipts-first way to read any gig app is the rate after hidden costs, the same math that prices bank account churning at $30-50/hr after taxes and ranks pallet runs by real take-home per hour. The largest hidden cost on a gig app is deactivation: payouts freeze, the account closes, and six weeks of pay is a realistic bill. A cost that size belongs inside the hourly rate, not in a story told after the fact.

Set the base first. Twenty hours a week at $25 an hour is $500 a week and $25,000 across a 50-week year, 1,000 hours of work. A deactivation lands as 6 x $500 = $3,000 of lost pay, which is 12 percent of the year's gig income in one email. The 72-hour response plan exists because the first reply usually decides the appeal, and the hours spent assembling receipts for it bill at zero.

Price the deactivation the way payroll risk is priced: as a premium. At 10 percent annual incidence the expected loss is 0.10 x $3,000 = $300, which spreads to $300 / 1,000 hours = $0.30 an hour, taking a $25 rate to $24.70. At 50 percent incidence the premium is $1.50 an hour and the rate falls to $23.50. The haircut is linear: double the incidence and the premium doubles, no raise negotiation required to lose a dollar an hour.

The response plan is the lever that moves the premium. If a same-day documented reply turns a six-week loss into a two-week loss, the expected loss at 50 percent incidence drops from $1,500 to $500, and the true hourly climbs from $23.50 to $24.50. An hour of receipt-keeping that cuts the expected loss by $1,000 a year is the best-paid hour on the platform, and it never appears on the task list.

The multi-app stack works the same arithmetic. Split the twenty hours across two apps at $250 a week each and a single deactivation exposes $1,500 instead of $3,000. With independent 50 percent risks both accounts die in the same year 0.5 x 0.5 = 25 percent of the time, so three years in four the worst case is $1,500 rather than $3,000. Stacking does not raise the expected rate; it cuts the tail, and the tail is what turns a side hustle into a debt.

Every platform quote is gross of this premium. Effective hourly, after the deactivation line, is the only number worth comparing, whether the work is deliveries, pallets, or client email hunting priced by real cost.
